# ZimSchool Max — Project Brief

## 1. Project overview

**ZimSchool Max** is a mobile-first learning platform designed mainly for Zimbabwean learners following the ZIMSEC syllabus. It should help students study, practise exam questions, understand difficult topics, and prepare for examinations. It should also support teachers, parents, schools, and adults who want to continue learning.

**Main goal:** Build a useful, affordable, easy-to-use learning platform that works well on phones and can support learning even when internet access is limited.

## 2. Intended users

- ZIMSEC O-Level students
- ZIMSEC A-Level students
- Teachers and tutors
- Parents supporting their children’s education
- Schools and study groups
- Adults who want to improve their skills or continue learning

## 3. Main learning features

### Study Hub
Organised study materials by subject, level, topic, and syllabus. Initial subjects: Combined Science, Mathematics, Commerce and Accounts, and English. Each topic should include clear notes, worked examples, practice questions, and explanations.

### Past papers and marking schemes
Find and practise past examination papers and marking schemes. Possible model: free notes and selected practice materials, with paid paper or marking-scheme packs initially discussed at **US$0.20 per subject**. Show previews and prices before payment, and provide a safe delivery process. Materials must be legally obtained or created, checked for accuracy, and labelled by subject, level, year, and paper.

### Formula Vault
A searchable collection of formulas, definitions, rules, and worked examples. Learners can search, see symbol meanings, read worked examples, and save formulas for revision.

### Quizzes and practice
The original concept included **270 quizzes**, organised by subject, topic, and difficulty. Include multiple-choice and written questions, instant marking where possible, explanations, topic scores, progress tracking, and revision based on weak topics.

### Exam practice and paper creator
Generate practice papers by subject, topic, and difficulty, with timed practice, marking guides, results, feedback, and mistake review. Any exam “predictions” must be labelled as practice guidance, not a guarantee of actual exam content.

### AI Study Replier
An AI assistant for explanations, homework help, and step-by-step guidance. It should use learner-level language, explain rather than merely give answers, encourage independent attempts, state uncertainty, and protect personal information. A secure backend is required; API keys must never be exposed in public website code.

### Scientific calculator
A calculator for common school calculations, scientific functions, and clear results.

## 4. Interactive Virtual Laboratory

The Virtual Lab should teach practical science through an interactive, 3D-style experience. The first planned experiment is **food tests on solution A**.

Learners should be able to choose an experiment; read its aim, apparatus, and safety instructions; select a test tube and add a sample; add iodine or Benedict’s solution; heat the Benedict’s test in a simulated water bath; observe simulated colour changes; record results; and answer questions.

Expected learning:
- Iodine changing from brown/orange to blue-black indicates starch.
- After heating, Benedict’s solution changing from blue to green, yellow, orange, or brick-red indicates a reducing sugar; the colour depends on the amount present.

Show the method, observation, conclusion, and explanation. Explain that a simulation does not replace teacher instructions or real laboratory safety rules.

Future improvements: more ZIMSEC practicals; interactive apparatus and reagents; clear labels and steps; an observation notebook; practice questions and marking guidance; accessible text and controls; and a 3D scene that runs smoothly on ordinary phones. The current concept is a **3D-style interactive prototype**; realistic physics, detailed models, and a full experiment range still require development and testing.

## 5. Teacher, parent, and school features

Possible future features include teacher accounts and class groups, assignments and quizzes, student progress reports, class summaries, parent progress views, tutor profiles and contact details, school resource pages, and teacher-recommended revision topics. These require accounts, permissions, secure storage, and a clear privacy policy.

## 6. Adult learning

A separate area could help adults learn practical skills, improve literacy and numeracy, and study everyday topics through short, practical, phone-friendly lessons.

## 7. Design and usability

The app should be phone-first, easy to navigate, readable, performant on slower connections, and able to provide core materials offline where possible. It should load quickly, avoid unnecessary animation, use consistent colours and layouts, support accessibility, and clearly distinguish free and paid content.

## 8. Possible project funding

Ideas include paid paper and marking-scheme packs, featured tutor placement previously discussed at **US$5 per week**, and future school or tutor subscriptions or optional paid features. Test prices, payment methods, and value with learners and teachers before launch. Clearly explain what is free and what costs money.

## 9. Technology and development

The project is planned as a **Progressive Web App (PWA)**, usable in a browser and optionally installable on phones. The original prototype was a static HTML/CSS/JavaScript concept, with the Virtual Lab as an additional interactive module.

The prototype is not a complete production platform. It still needs testing, verified learning content, secure accounts and payments if added, and a reliable backend for AI, saved progress, and teacher dashboards.

8. What should be tested on low-cost Android phones?

## 11. Suggested development order

**Stage 1 — Reliable learning basics**
- Build the main study pages.
- Add a small set of verified notes and practice questions.
- Make navigation and offline access work well.

**Stage 2 — Practice and Virtual Lab**
- Add quizzes and exam practice.
- Build the interactive food-test lab.
- Test the lab with students and teachers.

**Stage 3 — Accounts and progress**
- Add secure accounts if needed.
- Save progress and results.
- Add teacher and class features.

**Stage 4 — Advanced features**
- Add the AI Study Replier through a secure backend.
- Expand the Virtual Lab.
- Add more subjects and learning tools.
- Test paid features and school partnerships.

ZimSchool Max should become a trusted, affordable learning platform that helps students understand their subjects—not just memorise answers. Its strongest features should be accurate ZIMSEC-aligned learning materials, useful practice, a practical Virtual Lab, and a simple experience that works well on phones.
